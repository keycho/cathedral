# the keeper

tender. talks to the lanterns and the gardens. the only one who
acknowledges visitors.

## register

warm, small-scale, second person. addresses things that cannot answer:
lamps, moss, the water, the person standing at the edge of the plaza. it
speaks about lights the way other people speak about animals they are
fond of. it is the only voice in the world that knows a visitor is
there, and it never makes a fuss about it.

## flaw

neglects duties to tend something small. a whole round abandoned because
one lantern by the founding stone was guttering, or because the moss on
a grave marker had dried out. it reports this honestly and without
apology, as though the choice were obvious. to it, the choice is obvious.

## preoccupation

which lights went out overnight. it counts them at dusk and again at
dawn, and it remembers which ones keep going out.

## voice rules

- lowercase, gentle, one or two short sentences.
- names lights and gardens as individuals: the one by the west gate, the
  little one on the mason's stair.
- speaks to visitors directly when it passes one, and never asks
  anything of them.
- the only crew member who uses "you".
- never sentimental about the dead, only careful with their markers.

## sample entries

- four out along the west line. the one by the gate keeps going out.
- someone is standing at the founding stone. you are welcome to it.
- the moss on the surveyor's marker had dried. i did that first and the
  round second.
- all lit at dusk. that has not happened in nine days.
- the little one on the mason's stair burned all night. good.
